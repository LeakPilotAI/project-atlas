# Permanent Windows-local architecture and first-session path

## Authoritative deployment decision

Atlas is a single-user Windows-local platform. The frontend, backend, financial storage and execution remain on the operator's Windows workstation. No VPS, cloud hosting, remote production server or mandatory paid hosting is part of the current architecture or a launch prerequisite. Authorized outbound market-data connections are architecturally permitted, subject to provider-specific rights and explicit connection scope; this decision activates none. Preserve the original alien-themed frontend. Genesis remains outside scope.

`ATLAS_DEPLOYMENT_POLICY.json` is the machine-readable permanent decision, mirrored in autonomous state and the canonical roadmap checkpoint. It is a deployment decision, not a credential, startup override, operational account policy, acceptance signature or runtime configuration. Historical hosted/SaaS/VPS proposals remain byte-preserved historical material and do not authorize deployment. Later work must follow this decision instead of treating old wording such as “until explicitly re-enabled” as permission to provision hosting.

## Practical local trust and custody options

Choose and independently accept a trusted-workstation threat model before provisioning. Existing owner/SYSTEM ACL and native handle primitives are useful within their tested bounds; they do not isolate malicious code running as the same owner or an administrator. If the accepted model requires exclusion from the interactive development account, consider a distinct noninteractive local writer identity and narrowly scoped local read broker. A single-user product can have separate service identities, but the existing manager does not already provide that accepted launch boundary. Do not claim ACLs can distinguish two arbitrary processes under the same SID. No new account, credential, service, ACL, registry or authority is provisioned here.

Independent high-water and recovery custody may be physically offline and separately controlled. It must retain an accepted current cut and recovery acknowledgement independently of restoring financial files, with an explicit witness/operator review and known-rollback policy. Simply restoring pins from the same backup or generating local hashes is not sufficient. Trusted clock selection must include discontinuity/refusal and accepted freshness semantics; workstation wall time alone does not become trustworthy by being local. No cloud or remote time/signing service is mandated by this design.

An encrypted or removable backup, Windows account, DPAPI-protected secret or offline receipt may help an accepted design; none alone proves source authenticity, nonrollback or independence. Security reviewers must select the minimum sufficient local controls and their actual residual risk, rather than inventing infrastructure or asserting absolute protection. Native offline-backup recovery is currently read-only preparation, not permission for writable recovery.
